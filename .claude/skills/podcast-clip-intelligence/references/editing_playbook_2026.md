# Editing playbook — Shorts, Reels and trailers (research as of Oct 2026)

Research window: May–Oct 2026. Each rule is tagged by how solid it is:

- **[platform]** stated by YouTube/Instagram, reported consistently by trade press
- **[data]** a vendor or study with numbers (vendor data, not independent)
- **[practice]** editor consensus or a named creator's workflow — opinion, no measurement
- **[old: date]** older than the window; still used but re-check

Nothing here predicts views. Where no evidence was found, it says so. Re-check the platform facts
every few months; they change fast.

## 1. Platform facts that change what we cut

| Fact | Tag | What the skill does about it |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube (1 Oct 2026): Shorts recommendations "further prioritize original content" and reduce reach for re-uploads that add nothing. Voice-over that just describes the screen, minor technical edits and bulk template changes do **not** count as original; commentary, editing and storytelling do. | [platform] | Every Short gets an **added-value element** (context card, framing text hook, host reaction cut, chaptered storytelling). Clips posted from the podcast's own channel are the original; flag third-party clipper use. |
| YouTube inauthentic-content policy (YPP): near-identical titles, descriptions or tags across uploads and template sameness are red flags. | [platform, old: Jul 2025] | Write a distinct title, description and caption style choice per clip. Never batch-duplicate metadata. |
| YouTube auto-labels realistic AI/altered content on Shorts (late May 2026). | [platform] | Any realistic AI B-roll, AI voice or face edit is disclosed. Prefer real footage. |
| Shorts can be up to 3 min; YouTube gives no ideal length. Any start or replay counts as a view; only engaged views count for YPP. "Viewed vs. swiped away" in Studio is the hook check. | [platform] | Judge hooks by swipe-away rate after publishing, not by view count. |
| YouTube Shorts Series (23 Sep 2026): group Shorts into seasons/episodes with sequential playback. | [platform] | When one episode yields a multi-part story, propose it as a Series in Section G. |
| Instagram Reels can be 20 min, but Reels over 3 min aren't recommended to non-followers; Trial Reels go to non-followers. | [platform via blogs] | Keep Reels ≤ 3 min (our 90 s cap already does). Suggest Trial Reels to A/B a hook. |
| Instagram swaps reposts for the original in recommendations; repeat reposters are dropped from recommendations. | [platform via blogs] | Same added-value rule as YouTube. |
| Instagram's top signals: watch time, likes per reach, **sends per reach** (sends weigh most for non-follower reach). | [platform, old: Jan 2025] | Shareability scores "would someone forward this to a specific person?" |

## 2. Shorts / Reels — start to end

**First 1–3 seconds (hook)**

1. Start mid-thought on the claim itself — no "so…", no logo, no intro card, no setup question unless the
   question is the hook. **[data]** QuickReel's 10,000-clip analysis (2026) lists "starts mid-thought" and
   "duration tight to the moment" among the traits of clips that travel.
2. The first frame already shows the speaker's face and the first caption line. On-screen hook text
   (≤ 7 words) may restate the claim in the speaker's own words; never sharpen it beyond what was said.
3. Prefer moments that aren't already viral from the guest's other interviews. **[practice]** — Figuring Out
   (Raj Shamani) researches guests' past interviews to avoid saturated topics (Forbes, 7 Oct 2026).

**Middle (hold)**

4. Only the context the payoff needs. Cut repeats, false starts and filler, but review detected fillers before
   deleting so intentional pauses survive. **[practice]**
5. Change the picture at meaningful moments (a punch-in on the key line, a cut to the listener's reaction).
   No evidence supports a fixed "cut every N seconds" rule; cutting faster than the story carries hurts. **[practice]**

**End (payoff)**

6. End on the landed line. Shortest working cut wins. Default length 15–45 s; longer only when the payoff
   needs it. **[data, old: Nov 2025]** OpusClip data quoted second-hand: retention highest at 15–30 s, falling
   after ~45 s.
7. Loop endings, CTA vs no CTA: **no 2026 evidence found** — editorial choice, don't claim it helps.

**Captions**

8. Caption every spoken word. 5–7 words per line, white with dark stroke, one keyword per line in an accent
   colour, word-by-word highlight. The aggressive 2024 style (every word popping, all yellow, emoji spam) reads
   as dated. **[practice]**
9. Keep captions out of the top ~20 % and bottom ~25 % of a 9:16 frame (UI overlays). Figures vary by source —
   check with a test upload. **[practice]**
10. Hinglish: Roman script for young urban audiences; Devanagari for Hindi-belt and older audiences; a Roman
    hook line over a Devanagari body is a common mix. Auto-captions mishandle code-switching — a human pass is
    required. Engagement-lift claims for either script are unmeasured. **[practice]**

## 3. Trailers and episode cold opens

1. **Cold open = the question the episode answers, not a summary.** The first sentence is a specific claim,
   number or moment, cut in mid-sentence. No logo, sting or music swell before it. **[practice]**
2. **DOAC-style montage** (Diary of a CEO): counter-intuitive line first; music rises with the stakes, then
   drops to silence right before the key line; large keyword text; B-roll only where it adds evidence.
   **[practice]** — third-party breakdown, not official. No source found saying this pattern is stale; the
   criticism is of generic, AI-picked versions that all sound alike. Make every beat specific to this guest.
3. Length: hook beat under ~8 s; full cold-open montage under ~45 s; vertical teaser 15–30 s. **[practice]**
4. Title / show name after the hook and the first curiosity beat, never before the first line. **[practice]**
5. In long-form, reset the picture roughly every 90 s (wide shot, graphic, B-roll). Deliver at about -14 LUFS
   integrated. **[practice]**
6. **Never splice separate moments so they play as one continuous statement.** Mark the join visibly
   (hard cut to a different angle, a dip, a context card) and log it as non-chronological. **[platform/legal
   context]** — Oct 2026 defamation suit over a 42-second rearranged mash-up of three streams; NPR (May 2026) on
   the paid podcast-clipper wave.

## 4. Efficient workflow

1. Transcript first: pick and order moments from the transcript (this skill), then cut in the NLE. **[practice]**
2. Typical 2026 chain: record (e.g. Riverside) → transcript-based rough cut (Descript or Premiere text-based
   editing) → Premiere for multicam, colour, graphics, captions. Auto-multicam plugins (AutoPod, FireCut,
   EditBuddy) switch angles by who is speaking. **[practice]**
3. Build one caption style preset and one 9:16 reframe preset per show, then vary hook text, title and the
   added-value element per clip so uploads don't look templated (see section 1).
4. Cut all Shorts as separate sequences from one source sequence, named consistently (see the
   `premiere-podcast-cutter` skill), so revisions don't touch each other.

## 5. Stale or penalised — avoid

- Unchanged re-uploads and clip-farm reposts (reach cut on YouTube, removed from Instagram recommendations).
- Identical titles, tags or templates across uploads.
- Voice-over that only narrates what's on screen as the "added" value.
- Undisclosed realistic AI visuals or voices.
- Every-word-pops yellow captions, pointing-at-floating-text, staged reactions. **[practice]**
- Out-of-context splices and sensational headlines the speaker wouldn't endorse.

## Gaps (no reliable 2026 evidence found)

Hook-length timing benchmarks, cut frequency, loop endings, J/L-cut effects, split-screen vs single-speaker
framing, sound-design impact, number of trailer beats. Treat any rule about these as editorial taste.
